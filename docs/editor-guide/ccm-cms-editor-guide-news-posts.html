Editor's Guide

Publishing a News Post on the CCM Hub

A friendly, end-to-end walkthrough for editors who are brand-new to Sanity Studio. We'll start with the lay of the land, then build a news post from a blank page to a published article — paired side-by-side with the live site so you always know what each field actually does.

Promise: If you can fill in a form, you can publish on CCM. No code, no jargon. Just follow the ten steps in section 3.

How to open and read this file

This guide is a single self-contained HTML file. Nothing to install, nothing to download separately.

  1. Double-click editor-guide.html — it opens in your default browser (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge, all fine).
  2. Use the left sidebar to jump between sections, or just scroll top-to-bottom the first time.
  3. Want a PDF? In your browser press ⌘P (Mac) or Ctrl+P (Windows) and choose Save as PDF. The layout is print-friendly.

1 · How the CMS is organised

The CMS is built on Sanity Studio and lives at hub.connectingclimateminds.org/studio. When you log in, you see three columns: a navigation rail on the far left, a list of items in the middle, and the document you're editing on the right. Think of it as a filing cabinet — drawer, folder, document.

Sanity Studio · top-level structure

A The desk — top-level groups of content. News & Sources is where you live. B The list — every document of that type. Click one to open it, or hit + to make a new one. C The document — the actual fields you fill in.

The other drawers — what they're for (just so you know)

  • Homepage — the hero, intro and featured blocks on the front page.
  • Website — global pages like About, Contact, Footer text.
  • Research & Content — long-form research, frameworks, publications.
  • News & Sources — this is your home. Two sub-types: News Posts (articles we author) and External Sources (links to other people's articles).
  • Affiliations — partner organisations and how they appear on the site.
  • User Management — accounts that can log into the hub.
  • Communities & Users — Regional Communities and member profiles.
  • Taxonomies — the tags, regions and topics that classify everything. You'll pick from these, not edit them.
  • Support & Engagement — contact forms, FAQs, helpdesk content.
Mental model: the CMS is a database, but you don't think about databases. You think: which drawer, which folder, which fields. That's it.

3 · Step-by-step: your first News Post

From blank page to published article in ten small steps. Don't rush — every step has a "what this looks like on the site" panel so you can see the consequence of every click.

1
Open a fresh, blank post

In the left rail go to News & Sources → News Posts. At the top of the list, click the round + button. A new empty document called Untitled opens on the right.

What you'll see
Tip: notice the document is a draft until you publish. Drafts are private to editors — they never appear on the live site.
2
Write the Title (in every language you can)

The Title field is internationalised (i18n). You'll see four little language tabs — en, es, fr, ar. English is required; the others are optional but recommended. Click each tab and type the translated headline.

CMS · Title field with locale tabs
Public · article headline
Heads up: if a locale tab is empty, the site falls back to English. Empty translations aren't broken — they're just untranslated.
3
Generate the Slug

The slug is the URL-friendly name. Don't type it by hand — click the Generate button next to the field and Sanity will derive it from the English title. Edit only if you need to shorten it.

CMS · Slug
Public · the URL
Once published, don't change the slug. Changing it breaks every link and bookmark that points to the article.
4
Add Subtitle and Excerpt (both i18n)

Subtitle is one short sentence that appears under the headline. Excerpt is two-to-three sentences used in card previews on the news index page and in search results. Both are translatable — same four tabs as the title.

CMS · Subtitle & Excerpt
Public · article header + news card
5
Pick the Author and set Published At

Author is a reference field — start typing and pick from existing people. If your author doesn't exist yet, stop here and ask an admin to add them first (don't invent a new one inside this post). Published At is the date that shows on the byline; it also controls sort order on the news index.

CMS
Public · byline
6
Upload the Featured Image (with alt text!)

Drop an image into the Featured Image field — landscape, at least 1600 × 900 px, JPEG or PNG. After upload, click the image and fill in alt text. Alt text is read aloud by screen readers and shown if the image fails to load. It's not optional — write a one-sentence description of what the image shows.

CMS · image with alt
Public · hero + card
7
Link Projects, Regional Community and Location

These three fields connect the article to the rest of the hub. Projects — pick any CCM projects the post relates to. Regional Community — choose one (e.g. Africa, Latin America). Location — optional latitude/longitude if the story has a specific place; it makes the post appear as a pin on the regional map.

What the connections do
8
Add Region, Tags and Sources

Region and Tags come from the Taxonomies drawer — you pick from existing options, you don't type free text. They power the filter chips on the news index. Sources is a list of references; each one points at an External Source document (add those in News & Sources → External Sources first if they don't exist yet).

CMS
Public · news index filters
9
SEO — Open Graph image & No Index

Switch to the SEO tab at the top of the document. Open Graph Image is the preview that shows when someone shares the article on LinkedIn, X, WhatsApp. If you leave it empty, the Featured Image is used instead — that's usually fine. No Index is a checkbox; only tick it if you want to hide this post from Google (e.g. a private announcement).

CMS · SEO tab
Public · share preview
10
Preview, then Publish

Before you publish, click the eye / Preview icon in the top-right of the document. It opens the article on the live site in draft mode — looks exactly like the published version, but only you can see it. Check the headline, hero image, byline, and that links work. Happy? Hit the big green Publish button. The article goes live within seconds.

Action bar
Made a mistake after publishing? Just edit the document and hit Publish again — the live article updates in place. Want to unpublish? Open the document menu (•••) and choose Unpublish; the draft stays so you can re-publish later.

4 · How translations behave on the site

Every i18n field has four tabs: en, es, fr, ar. When a visitor switches the site language in the top-right menu, the page swaps to that locale's URL prefix (/en/, /es/, /fr/, /ar/) and re-renders using the matching tab of every i18n field.

If a tab is empty, the site falls back to English. So a Spanish visitor reading a half-translated post will see the navigation in Spanish but the article body in English. That's expected — it's better than a blank page — but it's also a signal to finish the translation.

EN · /en/news/your-slug
ES · /es/news/your-slug (partly translated)

Chrome (nav, footer, language switcher) is always localised. Article fields fall back per-field — so the title can be in ES even if the excerpt isn't yet.

Editor rule of thumb: when you publish, fill in at least en for everything. Then add es, fr, ar as translations arrive — you don't have to wait to have all four to publish.

6 · Pre-publish checklist

Run through this before you hit Publish. Five seconds each. Saves you from the most common embarrassment-after-go-live moments.

  • English Title, Excerpt and Featured Image are all filled in.
  • Slug is short, lowercase, hyphen-separated and matches the topic.
  • Featured Image has alt text written in plain language.
  • Author exists and is the correct person (not a placeholder).
  • Published At is set to the date you actually want the article to bear.
  • At least one Region or Tag is set so the post shows up in filters.
  • Sources list every external article or report you cited.
  • You clicked Preview and the page looks the way you expect.
  • If translations exist, the es/fr/ar tabs of Title and Excerpt are filled in.
  • No Index is unchecked (unless this post should be hidden from search).

7 · Common pitfalls (and how to avoid them)

"My post isn't on the news index"

Two usual suspects. Either the document is still a draft (look for the orange dot in the top bar — hit Publish), or Published At is set in the future. The index respects publish dates: a post dated next Tuesday won't appear until next Tuesday.

"The Spanish page shows English text"

That's the i18n fallback working as designed. Open the document, switch to the es tab on the field that's still in English, type the translation, publish again.

"The share preview on LinkedIn looks wrong / outdated"

Social platforms cache OG previews aggressively. Edit the post, make sure OG Image is set (or Featured Image is good), publish, then use LinkedIn's Post Inspector / X's Card Validator to force a refresh of their cache.

"I changed the slug and now an old link is broken"

Yep — that's why we say don't change the slug after publish. If you absolutely must, ask a developer to add a redirect from the old URL to the new one.

"I can't find the author I need"

Authors live in User Management. If your author isn't there, an admin needs to add them first. Don't be tempted to put the name in the Title or Subtitle as a workaround — the byline won't render.

"I accidentally deleted something"

Sanity keeps document history. Open the document, click the clock icon in the top-right, scroll to the version before the mistake, and click Restore. Nothing is ever truly gone for at least 30 days.

Stuck? Take a screenshot of the field giving you trouble and post it in the editors channel. Nine times out of ten it's a one-line fix.
